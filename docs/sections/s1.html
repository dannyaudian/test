Background / Deccription

Current conditions, the intended operating principles, and the four root causes holding back transaction flow from front to back.

A. Strategic Context — Administration Is the New Competitive Battlefield

Competition does not stop after a customer signs the SPK. Customers also judge a dealer based on:

  • Clarity of the payment process.
  • Speed of Sales Order creation.
  • Billing speed.
  • Certainty of unit readiness.
  • Delivery accuracy.
  • Clarity of STNK/BPKB status.
  • Transparency of transaction progress.
  • Consistency of information from Frontman through Administration.
  • Ease of obtaining and downloading the e-receipt.
Core Message

Customers do not see Sales, Finance, Administration, Treasury, and Logistics as separate functions. Customers only see one dealer experience.

FAST must ensure that experience is built through one integrated process, one and the same data source, and one transaction lineage that connects:

B. Current Condition

From the outside, the customer journey looks simple:

SPK→Booking Fee/DP→Sales Order→Settlement→Billing→Unit Preparation→Delivery→Document Completion

However, the process behind it involves:

  • Customer data validation.
  • Document checking.
  • Booking fee/DP request.
  • Booking fee/DP verification.
  • Sales Order generation.
  • One-time or staged settlement.
  • Payment allocation per unit or Sales Order.
  • Receipt issuance.
  • Outstanding balance monitoring.
  • Compliance validation.
  • Compliance exception routing.
  • Billing readiness.
  • AFI/DO processing.
  • Account Receivable clearance.
  • VIN and PDI validation.
  • Delivery authorization.
  • Good Issue.
  • STNK/BPKB monitoring.
  • Customer status notification.

Currently, the same data can potentially be requested or re-entered by different functions at different stages. For example:

  • Customer data is entered by Frontman, then entered again by Administration.
  • STNK name and address are re-entered in the vehicle document process.
  • Unit data is re-entered in Sales Order, billing, AFI, or DO.
  • The relationship between the SPK number, Sales Order number, and billing number is not yet always visible as one transaction lineage.
  • Payment data is checked and re-allocated manually.
  • The payment reference is not yet always automatically connected to the receipt number and transaction documents.
  • Receipts are not always available as e-receipts customers can download.
  • The unit outstanding does not yet always account for active receipts and in-progress payment requests in real time.
  • Compliance checking results and supporting proof are resubmitted when an exception needs to be raised.
  • Delivery data is re-matched against data already available on the SPK or Sales Order.
  • Customers do not yet have one home to view order status, unlocked invoices, payment requests, e-receipts, blockers, and next actions.
  • The unit handover proof is not yet an authentic pack that is the same for the branch and the customer (photo, VIN, geotag, timestamp, recipient, BSTKB). What is left is often only a staff claim or dealer CCTV footage that cannot be used.

Public case: already paid, unit not handed over

A recurring pattern in public spaces: the customer has already paid, the unit is never handed over, staff can deny it, and dealer camera footage does not serve as reliable proof. The customer then records it themselves or takes the matter to the legal route. A circulating example: an upload of a follow-up inspection of a car purchase that was already paid for but not handed over (third party; not a FAST transaction, not a FAST brand or branch). FAST does not copy that video. What is taken from it is the risk pattern.

Why FAST

Unlocked status and a digital proof pack replace “trusting the staff / checking the CCTV.” Cash settlement remains non-waivable. After the unit is actually handed over, Good Issue records proof the customer can download — so the branch cannot deny the handover, and the customer does not have to record it secretly.

That condition creates:

  • Redundant input.
  • Data discrepancies between processes.
  • Rechecking.
  • Rework.
  • Risk of input errors.
  • Risk of duplicate payment requests.
  • Risk of overpayment.
  • Receipts and payments are hard to trace.
  • Dependence on manual communication.
  • Longer processing time.
  • Customer uncertainty.
  • Units paid for but not handed over, without a proof pack that the customer and branch see as the same.

C. Future Operating Principle

In the target condition, each piece of data is entered only once, at the first most relevant point, by the party that holds the most valid source of information. That data is then:

  1. Stored in one controlled data source.
  2. Validated by the system and the relevant functions.
  3. Reused across all subsequent processes.
  4. Carried from the front process to the back process.
  5. Not re-typed by the next function.
  6. Only completed when there is new data.
  7. Only corrected at the original data source.
  8. Given an audit trail whenever a change occurs.
  9. Connected to all transaction document numbers.
  10. Able to be displayed consistently to the Frontman, Administration, management, and customer.

Target Data Flow

Frontman Input→SPK No.→Payment + E-Receipt→SO No.→AFI→Billing No.→Delivery→Good Issue→STNK/BPKB
Core Principle

Input once at the source, validate at the right control point, and reuse throughout the entire transaction lifecycle.

D. Ideal Frontman-Driven Process

In the ideal condition, the Frontman can drive the entire process through one working tool by:

  1. Entering customer and transaction data.
  2. Uploading all initial documents.
  3. Sending or monitoring the booking fee payment request that is automatically billed after the SPK is saved; the amount follows the unit.
  4. Monitoring booking fee verification as a requirement for Sales Order creation.
  5. Viewing the e-receipt issued after the payment is verified.
  6. Submitting the Sales Order after the unit's minimum booking fee is verified.
  7. Sending the settlement payment request.
  8. Viewing AR Open per unit or Sales Order.
  9. Viewing payment requests that are still active.
  10. View Available Request Amount.
  11. Monitoring one-time or staged payment.
  12. Viewing payment allocation and outstanding balance.
  13. Submitting a compliance exception when a gate fails and the rule is declared waivable.
  14. Submitting the AFI (STNK name, STNK address, special requests) before billing.
  15. Submits billing-readiness request.
  16. Submits delivery request.
  17. Completing the Good Issue proof.
  18. Monitoring STNK/BPKB.
  19. Viewing requirements that are not yet complete.
  20. Viewing compliance exception and escalation status.

The Frontman does not need to re-enter data that has already been received.

Administration Role

Administration does not re-copy the Frontman's data. Administration's role is to:

  • View the list of SPK, quotation, SO, DO, AFI, billing, receipt, and branch payments from the same panel.
  • Verify data.
  • Check completeness.
  • Add internal data that is genuinely not yet available.
  • Handle unmatched payments.
  • Ensure transaction document relationships stay consistent.
  • Handle document or vehicle mismatch.
  • Ensure compliance.
  • Verify compliance exception evidence.
  • Control process continuation.
  • Make corrections at the original data source.

System Role

  • Distinguish data entered by the Frontman, Administration, the system, and the Compliance Exception Approver.
  • Validate data completeness.
  • Prevent duplicate input.
  • Carry data from one process to the next.
  • Connect the SPK, Sales Order, and billing numbers.
  • Generate the payment reference.
  • Generate one receipt number for every verified payment.
  • Allocate payments automatically.
  • Calculate AR Open.
  • Calculate Available Request Amount.
  • Control so that payment requests do not exceed the available outstanding.
  • Provide the e-receipt for the customer.
  • Open the next process when requirements are complete.
  • Hold the process when there is a shortfall.
  • Route a waivable failed compliance rule to the authorized party.
  • Record all changes and decisions in the audit trail.

E. Four Root Problems

Problem 1

Fragmented Process

SPK, booking fee/DP, Sales Order, settlement, compliance checking, exception decision, billing, delivery, and document tracking still move through many functions and applications.

Impact

  • Many manual handoffs.
  • Process ownership is unclear.
  • Risk of the process stalling between functions.
  • Communication errors.
  • Resolution depends on individuals.
  • The Frontman must follow up with many parties.
  • Transaction status is not always visible in real time.
Problem 2

Manual Control

Compliance validation and exception decisions are still largely done through manual checking, confirmation, and follow-up.

Impact

  • The process moves only after someone checks it.
  • There is no consistent request-to-next-process flow.
  • Payment allocation risks being late.
  • Receipts risk being issued late.
  • Receipts are not yet always connected to the payment reference and transaction documents.
  • Outstanding balance is hard to monitor.
  • Compliance exceptions and escalations are unstructured.
  • The audit trail is not always formed automatically.
  • The risk of bypass and inconsistency increases.
Problem 3

Redundant Input and Unclear Data Ownership

There is not yet a firm mapping of:

  • Data entered by the Frontman.
  • Data verified by Administration.
  • New data that Administration may add.
  • Data generated automatically by the system.
  • Decisions that fall under the Compliance Exception Approver's authority.
  • The system that serves as the source of truth.
  • The downstream processes that use that data.
  • The relationship between the SPK, Sales Order, billing, payment reference, and receipt numbers.

Impact

  • The same data is entered several times.
  • Data discrepancies between applications.
  • Administration performs re-entry.
  • Risk of typos and mismatch.
  • Processing time becomes longer.
  • Data changes do not always flow to the next process.
  • It is hard to determine who is responsible for data quality.
Problem 4

Limited Visibility

The Frontman, branch, management, and customer do not yet have one real-time transaction view.

Impact

  • Customers repeatedly ask about status.
  • The Frontman chases Administration and Finance.
  • Management learns of bottlenecks late.
  • Outstanding balance is not always clearly visible.
  • The Frontman does not know why the process is on hold.
  • Compliance exception status is not transparent.
  • Customers cannot yet see all unit invoices that are still open.
  • Customers cannot yet download all e-receipts from one place.
  • Customers do not yet have a complete order journey.
  • Customers do not see delivery blockers (for example, not yet paid in full) and do not have an authentic handover proof pack.

F. Business Impact

  • Slow SPK-to-SO processing.
  • Delayed booking fee/DP verification.
  • Delayed payment allocation.
  • Delayed receipt issuance.
  • Delayed billing.
  • Delayed cash-in recognition.
  • Delayed delivery.
  • Data and document mismatch.
  • Repeated data entry.
  • Broken transaction references.
  • Difficulty monitoring Account Receivable.
  • Risk of duplicate payment requests.
  • Risk of overpayment.
  • A large amount of manual follow-up.
  • Late compliance exception decisions.
  • Difficulty monitoring staged payments.
  • Low information certainty for the customer.
  • Control and customer experience are hard to improve at the same time.

G. Business Problem Statement

Problem statement

Current finance and administration processes still depend on fragmented handoffs, manual verification, repeated data entry, and manual status follow-up. Data ownership between Frontman and Administration has not been clearly mapped, while SPK, Sales Order, billing, payment, and receipt references are not always managed as one connected transaction lineage.

Information captured at the front is not yet consistently carried through Sales Order, billing, AFI/DO, delivery, Good Issue, and document completion. Customers also do not yet have one consolidated view of their open unit bills, active payment requests, downloadable e-receipts, outstanding balance, order status, blocker, next action, authentic handover evidence, and end-to-end journey.

Closing message

When process, payment, document lineage, and data ownership are disconnected, the transaction cannot flow seamlessly from front to back.
